# Practitioner Foot-Measurement Protocols for Bespoke Last Inputs

## 1. Verified landmarks and tape paths

### Takeaway

Practitioner girths are not one standard set of transverse slices. Some are shortest-path tape-contact loops; others are defined as plane-based circumferences. Record landmarks and the actual tape route separately from any later geometric section interpretation.

### Cited findings

| Measure | Verified protocol and limits |
|---|---|
| **Ball/joint** | 3DShoemaker passes the tape through the sides of the first and fifth ball joints and follows the shortest path around the foot. It specifies neither a planar cut nor that the MTP heads share a longitudinal station. [3DShoemaker, “Foot Girth Measurements,” Ball Girth](https://3dshoemaker.com/foot-girth-measurements-for-bespoke-and-custom-shoe-lasts/) Wayman’s remote form instead says wrap around the widest part, from big-toe joint to the bulge at the side of the little toe; it labels this A, “Joint Measurement,” and asks the user to mark tape position/angle. No plane is specified. [Wayman, *Bespoke Measurement Guide*, p. 3/5](https://www.waymanbespoke.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Intern.-Measurement-Guide.pdf) |
| **Waist/mid** | 3DShoemaker places waist girth halfway between its ball- and instep-girth measurements (“the plane of measurement is averaged”). Wayman B is exactly between its A and C measures. These are midpoint rules, not palpated landmarks; the sources do not establish that their endpoints or paths are equivalent. [3DShoemaker, Waist Girth](https://3dshoemaker.com/foot-girth-measurements-for-bespoke-and-custom-shoe-lasts/); [Wayman, p. 3/5](https://www.waymanbespoke.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Intern.-Measurement-Guide.pdf) |
| **Instep** | 3DShoemaker palpates the middle-cuneiform prominence (dorsal bony ridge) and loops through it around the plantar foot by the shortest path. It does not specify a plane. [3DShoemaker, Instep Girth](https://3dshoemaker.com/foot-girth-measurements-for-bespoke-and-custom-shoe-lasts/) Wayman C follows the narrowest route from mid-arch to a point about 2 cm above the “centre of the instep bone,” described as a bump to feel. This endpoint is not anatomically resolved; do not assume it is the middle cuneiform. [Wayman, p. 3/5](https://www.waymanbespoke.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Intern.-Measurement-Guide.pdf) |
| **Heel width** | Wayman asks for the heel’s widest width, without specifying a station. [Wayman, p. 3/5](https://www.waymanbespoke.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Intern.-Measurement-Guide.pdf) 3DShoemaker’s tracing-based width is different: perpendicular to a heel axis at 1/6 foot length from the heel point, between medial/lateral outline intersections. It is linear width, not heel girth. [3DShoemaker, “Getting Foot Measurements from a Foot Tracing,” Heel Width](https://3dshoemaker.com/getting-foot-measurements-from-a-foot-tracing/) |
| **Short heel** | In a documented bespoke fitting, maker Amara Hark Weber’s “short heel” is described as going around the back of the heel to the “magic spot,” where leg meets foot. The account gives no precise palpable point, plantar landmark, or plane. [Sven Raphael Schneider, “Measuring Feet for Bespoke Shoes,” Step Three](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/measuring-bespoke-shoes/) |
| **Long heel / “long instep”** | Wayman D starts just above the centre of the instep bone, wraps around the heel where it touches the ground, and returns to the start; the form calls it “Long Heel Measurement.” [Wayman, p. 3/5](https://www.waymanbespoke.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Intern.-Measurement-Guide.pdf) Amara’s long heel is the same style as short heel, but closer to instep. [Gentleman’s Gazette, Step Three](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/measuring-bespoke-shoes/) Neither source fixes a plane or angle. No inspected practitioner defines “long instep” as a separate standardized loop. |

IEEE’s compilation shows why names alone are unsafe: it calls long heel “instep-to-heel girth” and lists definitions through landing point plus an instep point at 50% or 55% foot length, perpendicular to a specified foot axis; some TechMed definitions instead incline the section 35° from horizontal. Short-heel definitions also vary: shortest circumference through the landing point perpendicular to an axis, or routes through a “tentative junction” point defined by ankle skin crease and toe-extensor tendon. These are compiled plane/landmark definitions, not proof that hand-tape paths are planar or that the practitioner “magic spot” is that junction. [IEEE SA, *Comprehensive Review of Foot Measurements Terminology in Use* (2021), §§3.2.3–3.2.4, 3.3.20–3.3.21, pp. 13–14, 47–49](https://standards.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/import/governance/iccom/3DBP-Foot_Measurements_Terminology.pdf)

### Inferences and gaps

- The MTP1/MTP5 points may be at different longitudinal stations. Forcing the shortest tape-contact path through them into a plane orthogonal to the foot/last axis would be a modeling interpretation, not the cited manual protocol.
- No inspected practitioner source fixes universal posterior/plantar heel, ankle-crease, “magic spot,” or exact tape-angle landmarks for manual short/long-heel wraps. The waist sources likewise do not specify a unique 3D loop.
- IEEE’s review states source definitions and methods are often incomplete and same names may mean different things. It is an industry-connections review, not a consensus standard; it expressly excludes last measurements. [IEEE SA, §§4.1–4.2, pp. 59–60](https://standards.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/import/governance/iccom/3DBP-Foot_Measurements_Terminology.pdf)

## 2. Conditions, hand tools, and foot-versus-last

### Takeaway

These are conditional descriptions of a **foot**, not instructions to copy foot girths directly onto a last. Makers differ in loading, socks, timing, and tape tension; preserve the procedure with the number.

### Cited findings

- **3DShoemaker:** soft tailor’s tape, helper recommended, tape end finger-pinned/read in mm; barefoot and seated semi-weightbearing, with intended socks accounted for separately. [Opening instructions](https://3dshoemaker.com/foot-girth-measurements-for-bespoke-and-custom-shoe-lasts/)
- **Wayman remote form:** printed templates, flexible tape, thin pencil/pen, helper for tracing; measure both feet standing, in the intended socks, in the afternoon, with tape snug to the wearer’s preferred semi-bespoke fit. Mark tape position/angle. [*Bespoke Measurement Guide*, pp. 1–3/5](https://www.waymanbespoke.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Intern.-Measurement-Guide.pdf)
- **Amara Hark Weber fitting:** seated upright, hip/knee/ankle at 90°, before noon, intended socks; tape not too tight. Same tape measures were repeated with the foot raised; full-weight pedograph was a distinct later procedure. [Gentleman’s Gazette, Steps One, Three, Five](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/measuring-bespoke-shoes/)
- **Patrick Frei fitting:** sitting and standing, with pressure varied by measure; he sometimes tightens then releases tape and asks when discomfort stops, as an individualized tolerance check. This is one maker’s practice, not a standard. [Jesper Ingevaldsson, “Report – Patrick Frei bespoke, part 1,” measurement section](https://shoegazing.com/2019/04/12/report-patrick-frei-bespoke-part-1/)
- Paper tracing, pencil, flexible tape, and ruler support linear foot lengths/widths without a scanner or contour gauge; 3DShoemaker’s manual workflow uses tape for girths and tracing for linear measures. [3DShoemaker, “Getting Foot Measurements from a Foot Tracing”](https://3dshoemaker.com/getting-foot-measurements-from-a-foot-tracing/); [Wayman guide](https://www.waymanbespoke.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Intern.-Measurement-Guide.pdf)
- 3DShoemaker says last measurements need allowances because the last’s sharp feather edge creates space unlike the foot; arch/instep space also depends on arch height. Sock/insert thickness and footwear purpose matter. No universal reduction or fill factor is given. [3DShoemaker, “Allowances for Custom Shoe Lasts,” Feather Edge Compensation, Instep Girth Fillup](https://3dshoemaker.com/allowances-for-custom-shoe-lasts/)

### Inferences and gaps

Conflicts: seated/barefoot versus standing/socked, before-noon versus afternoon, and unspecified versus fit-preference tape tension. Do not average them into one “correct” size. Sources give no common tension/repeat protocol or seated-to-standing conversion; infer no universal deformation or last reduction.

## 3. Researcher proposal, source ledger, and open questions

### Proposed minimum practical capture (my proposal, not source instruction)

For each foot, store raw named measure, landmarks, tape route including plantar contact, whether the source calls it shortest-path or plane-based, posture/load, barefoot/sock/orthotic state, tension instruction, date/time, and operator. Sketch/photograph the tape in place; do not retain only the result. Keep ball/joint, selected instep and waist protocols, heel width station, and each heel loop under the maker’s own name. Use flexible tape, paper tracing, pencil, and ruler for no-scan intake; repeat only under the same stated conditions and preserve both readings. Keep foot measures separate from any later last translation; do not apply a generic allowance from these sources.

### Open questions

- Which posterior/plantar and dorsal heel landmarks should the project adopt, and should it retain maker tape-contact paths or define a plane-based surrogate?
- Does any intended practitioner use “long instep” for a different loop from “long heel”? Not verified. IEEE’s compiled “instep-to-heel” synonym does not prove common practitioner usage.
- Which posture, sock/orthotic condition, and tape tension should be canonical for the target use? The reviewed practitioners differ; choose explicitly with practitioner/user input.
