# Last section measurement evidence: stations, planes, and boundaries

## Reference identification and source ledger

### Takeaway

The requested chapter title is real, but there are two editions to keep separate. The fully verifiable citation is the 2013 first-edition chapter *Shoe-last design templates*; the 2021 second edition also catalogs a chapter under that exact title, but its chapter-level metadata could not be independently completed from accessible publisher content. The material available without a paywall supports design curves, last orientations, and familiar girth locations; it does **not** establish a universal cross-section station schedule or a universal section-plane orientation.

## What the sources support: measurements versus design stations

### Takeaway

The sources distinguish *where a girth is measured* from *which cross-sections must define the last*. Ball, waist, and instep are defensible anatomical/fit measurement names, not proof of three equally spaced, transverse CAD planes. The primary chapter’s abstract says it treats “main design curves” and presents different heel heights and orientations, but the available excerpt does not name cross-section stations, give landmark paths/angles, or prescribe a world-vertical versus local-normal section frame. [PolyU abstract](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/shoe-last-design-templates)

The anatomical ball is associated with the metatarsal heads/joints, but this review found no accessible cited source specifying a third landmark or a rule that turns first/fifth-head landmarks into a uniquely oriented CAD plane. [Wang abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0166361510000205) | A plane perpendicular to the last centerline, a world-vertical plane, or a plane through both MTP heads are not interchangeable. **Foot/last geometry boundary (explicit distinction, not a quoted book rule).** A foot outside-plan tracing, a loaded plantar contact print, and a last’s bottom perimeter/feather line are different observables. The first is a projected outline; the second records contact under a loading condition; the third is a designed edge where upper meets bottom. They cannot be treated as the same curve without a stated transformation. A foot width or girth gives a scalar constraint, not the feather-edge shape or a dorsal cross-section. A scan can supply observed outer surface/profile data, but if the input is only a tracing, tape measurements, or loaded footprint, medial/lateral asymmetry, dorsum section, arch-lower curvature, and the alignment assumptions remain unobserved. These are geometric implications of the different measurement objects; the accessible chapter abstract and standard metadata do not prescribe their conversion.

**Longitudinal controls.** The chapter’s accessible abstract confirms that last design includes main design curves and different orientations; it does not reveal the curve definitions. At minimum, longitudinal side-profile and bottom-profile references should remain explicit and separate from any section schedule: last/foot centerline or other declared longitudinal datum, heel and toe extents, bottom/ground reference, toe spring/heel configuration where relevant, and medial/lateral outline features. Do not infer the upper reference or feather edge from a foot plantar tracing alone. The paper’s abstract-level research on fit also treats a last as more than a single width/length fit value. [PolyU abstract](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/shoe-last-design-templates); [Wang abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0166361510000205)

## Bounded station/control proposal and unresolved points

### Takeaway

**Proposal, not a rule quoted from any book or standard:** capture only stations justified by observations or design decisions, and store their landmark path and orientation explicitly. Do not turn the familiar girth names into six universal orthogonal ellipses.

### Minimal proposed control set

- **Forepart:** a ball/joint fit record tied to first- and fifth-metatarsal-head landmarks (and any third constraint explicitly declared by the measurement protocol); preserve the measured tape loop separately from any derived CAD section. Where landmark positions differ longitudinally, represent that anatomy rather than forcing both heads onto a made-up common transverse plane.
- **Midfoot:** retain waist and instep as separate *optional* constraints only when there are two observed or specified values/paths. Record where the tape passes over the dorsum and under the sole. If only one value exists, do not manufacture another station by interpolation and label it observed.
- **Rearfoot:** record rear heel extent/profile and the selected short-/long-heel tape path(s) as named path constraints. Do not use them as horizontal ring sections unless an independent convention explicitly defines the plane.
- **Toe and upper edge:** record toe extent and style-specific toe shape/height separately from fit girths; record topcut/topline only for the chosen last/footwear style, with its path and reference. Neither is a universal anatomical ring.
- **For every actual section:** record station source (landmark observation, measured tape, scan-derived, or design-selected), longitudinal location relative to a named datum, and section orientation. Explicitly distinguish world-vertical, normal-to-local-centerline, and landmark-defined/oblique planes; no reviewed source supplies an angle rule that permits substituting one for another.

### Gaps

- **Primary-reference limitation:** only metadata and abstract could be verified for the 2013 chapter; second-edition chapter-level author, pages, DOI, figures, and body are unresolved because Elsevier sign-in blocked access. Therefore, no detailed station or orientation claim is attributed to the chapter.
- **Plane and third-landmark rule:** no accessible primary footwear source located here gives a reproducible third ball landmark/constraint plus a plane-angle convention for a section through MTP1/MTP5. Do not infer a third point or force equal longitudinal coordinates.
- **Footprint/feather translation:** the sources accessed do not publish a universal mapping among a standing tracing, loaded print, anatomical scan, and last bottom/feather edge. Treat them as distinct inputs with any conversion labeled as a design assumption.
- **Terminology:** the chapter abstract does not define topcut, topline, short heel, long heel, or heel girth in full. An independent practitioner/clinical source with explicit landmark paths would be needed to compare those vocabularies; no equivalence is asserted here.
- **Formal standard:** ISO 19409:2022 is a relevant title-level reference, but the standard text must be consulted before claiming a standardized last-dimension station list or procedure.
