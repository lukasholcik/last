# Source verification record

Research and direct source checks were performed by read-only librarian lanes; this file archives their verification result. The report is a bounded research synthesis, not a systematic review or fit validation.

- **Smith et al. (2019), DOI 10.1016/j.jmbbm.2019.04.012:** PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31054375/ indexed original abstract and metadata checked. Confirms 10-person sample, passive static postures, 11×4 marker grid, and plantar results 5 ± 1% stretch in dorsiflexion versus 9 ± 2% relaxation/retraction in plantar flexion. Publisher redirected to authentication; do not claim full-article verification. Neither condition is a standing heel-height dose-response or heel-to-toe tape-path result.
- **Kouchi & Tsutsumi (2000), DOI 10.1537/ase.108.331:** Original J-STAGE PDF checked, https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ase1993/108/4/108_4_331/_pdf . Pages 333–337 confirm 39 adult female participants, 0/4/8 cm simulated heel elevations, optical dorsum measurement plus alginate plantar impression. Medial arch-length definition p. 336 is measured parallel to the X-axis, not skin-following. Reference plane rotates with the foot; it is horizontal only in the flat condition. No heel-to-toe surface-path outcome was verified.
- **Wan et al. (2017), DOI 10.1016/j.measurement.2016.12.005:** Institutional record and accepted manuscript checked, https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/handle/10397/65812 . Methods confirm 45 participants and 0/5/10 cm conditions. Manuscript p. 20 says the foot plantar is not scanned. A later sentence says 50 subjects, an internal sample-count inconsistency; methods/abstract use 45.
- **Zhu & Lee (2023), DOI 10.1186/s40691-023-00347-7:** Original open PDF checked, https://d-nb.info/1304595374/34 . Confirms 35 participants, wedge-height combinations, 15° toe spring, static scanning, and conventional dimension outcomes. No flat baseline or plantar tape-path result.
- **Sun et al. (2022), DOI 10.3389/fbioe.2022.959807:** Original full article checked. CT/bone-motion reconstruction estimates internal fascia path, not skin.
- **Wang et al. (2021), DOI 10.3389/fbioe.2021.791238:** Original full article checked, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8720874/ . Single-subject internal fascia simulation, not external skin or surface-path validation. Do not confuse relative peak-strain changes with foot growth.
- **LutraCAD:** Official https://academy.lutracad.com/cad/how-to/shoe-lasts/ checked for Rotate/Correct wording. Rotate leaves foot unmodified; Correct adjusts model to desired lift. No public scalar skin compensation was verified. Related scan/template workflow: https://academy.lutracad.com/cad/design/shoe-lasts/base/ .
- **3DShoemaker context:** https://3dshoemaker.com/measurements-in-shoe-last-maker/ distinguishes last-bottom profile length, functional/full length, and template heel-height grading. https://3dshoemaker.com/allowances-for-custom-shoe-lasts/ discusses toe/style allowances, not a verified heel-dependent skin correction. Those concepts are not interchangeable.

The user's greater-than-1-cm tape difference is a personal observation along the underside. Exact direction, height, loading, endpoints, and tape tension were not established. Published projected-length changes do not refute it, and it does not independently establish skin-material strain or a general last correction.
